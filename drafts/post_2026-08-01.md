Real-time carrier capacity management isn't just about sending an EDI 204. It's about integrating dynamic tender acceptance (EDI 990) directly into WMS outbound planning to prevent shipment delays and costly re-tenders.

Many systems treat 𝗘𝗗𝗜 𝟮𝟬𝟰/𝟵𝟵𝟬 as a fire-and-forget process. This often leads to WMS over-promising carrier capacity, resulting in missed pickups, expedited freight, or manual intervention. The true challenge lies in making the WMS *responsive* to real-time carrier commitments, or lack thereof. Without immediate feedback, planned shipments become speculative.

Implement a hybrid strategy. Use APIs for initial tender creation and real-time status checks where available. Supplement with EDI 204 for tender transmission and 𝗘𝗗𝗜 𝟵𝟵𝟬 for formal acceptance. Crucially, integrate 𝗘𝗗𝗜 𝟵𝟵𝟬 𝗮𝗰𝗸𝗻𝗼𝘄𝗹𝗲𝗱𝗴𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁𝘀 back into WMS slotting algorithms and ERP freight accruals *immediately*. Prioritize integrations that push rejected tenders back to a re-tender queue or alternative carrier selection process automatically. This keeps the outbound flow agile.
