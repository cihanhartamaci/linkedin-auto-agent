High-scale logistics processes rarely achieve true atomicity across disparate WMS, OMS, and ERP systems. Relying on traditional distributed transactions is often impractical and introduces single points of failure.

The challenge intensifies when a multi-step workflow – say, an order fulfillment journey from OMS to WMS to ERP invoicing – fails mid-process. Simple retries or Dead Letter Queues are insufficient. We need a robust strategy to maintain business data consistency by effectively "undoing" prior successful steps. This is where 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗮𝘁𝗼𝗿𝘆 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗻𝘀𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝘀 and the 𝗦𝗮𝗴𝗮 𝗽𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗻 become critical architectural components.

To implement Sagas effectively:
*   Define each workflow step as an independent, 𝗶𝗱𝗲𝗺𝗽𝗼𝘁𝗲𝗻𝘁 local transaction within its system.
*   Design a specific "compensation" action for each step that can reverse its effect.
*   Utilize an orchestrator (centralized) or choreography (decentralized) pattern to manage the sequence. This component triggers compensation actions if any step fails, ensuring eventual consistency without holding locks across systems.
